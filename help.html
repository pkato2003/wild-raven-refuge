Found a bird?

The first hour
matters most.

Read this before you do anything. Most of what people instinctively do for a downed bird makes things worse.

Call (541) 214-0198

If the bird has been in a cat’s mouth, call us immediately. Cat bites are the most time-critical thing we see. Twelve hours is the difference between treatment and euthanasia.

Do these four things

In this order.

  1. 01

    Put it in a box. Dark, quiet, warm.

    A cardboard box with air holes punched in the lid. A folded towel or an old t-shirt on the bottom — something with no loose threads, because birds get their toes caught in loops. Close the lid.

    Put the box somewhere dark, quiet and warm. A bathroom with the door shut works well. So does a garage, as long as it’s above 60°F.

    No radio. No television. No children. No pets in the room. A bird in shock can die from stress alone.

  2. 02

    Do not feed it. Do not give it water.

    This is the single hardest instruction to follow, and the most important.

    A dehydrated or shocky bird cannot digest food. Food given at this stage sits in the crop and rots. Water is worse — birds don’t drink the way we do, and liquid poured into a bird’s mouth almost always ends up in the lungs. That’s aspiration pneumonia, and it kills within days.

    A bird can safely go 24 hours without food. It cannot safely go 24 hours with fluid in its lungs.

  3. 03

    Don’t try to fix anything yourself.

    No splints. No bandages. No antiseptic creams. No trying to set a wing.

    A wing splinted incorrectly for three days will fuse incorrectly, and that bird will never fly again. A leg wrapped too tight loses circulation in about four hours. And most over-the-counter antiseptics are toxic to birds.

    Keep the bird contained and leave the medicine to someone with a licence and an X-ray machine.

  4. 04

    Call us. Or call someone.

    (541) 214-0198, any hour. If we can’t take the bird, we’ll tell you who can. We keep a current list of every licensed rehabber in western Oregon and we hand it out freely.

    If you’re outside Lane County, call your nearest wildlife rehabilitation centre first. In Oregon, ODFW’s Salem office can also point you to someone.

Before you pick it up

Is it actually orphaned? Probably not.

Every spring and summer we get calls about “orphaned” crow fledglings hopping around on the ground. The overwhelming majority of them are not orphaned. They are doing exactly what crow chicks are supposed to do.

Corvid chicks leave the nest about a week before they can fly. They spend that week on the ground, in bushes, and on low branches, while their parents feed them every few hours and scream at anything that comes near. It looks alarming. It is completely normal.

How to tell the difference

Leave it alone if…

  • It’s fully feathered, with a short tail and stubby wings
  • It can hop, and it’s holding its head up
  • There’s an adult crow or raven nearby, watching you
  • Adults are bringing it food
  • It’s moving around a bush or low branch

Keep cats and dogs indoors for about ten days. That’s the whole intervention.

Call us if…

  • It’s naked, or covered in down but no real feathers
  • It’s lying flat and can’t right itself
  • No adult has appeared in two hours of watching
  • There’s visible blood, a drooping wing, or a bad leg
  • The nest has fallen and the parents are gone
  • It was brought in by a cat or dog

Put it in a box as described above and call us. Don’t wait to see if it improves.

Handling

How to pick up a corvid without hurting it or yourself.

A crow can break a finger. A raven can break two. Be deliberate, be quick, and don’t grab.

  1. Wear gloves if you have them

    Leather gardening gloves are ideal. If you don’t have gloves, use a thick towel.

  2. Approach from behind, not the front

    Their beak is the dangerous end. Coming from behind reduces the chance of a bite.

  3. Drop a towel over the bird first

    Darkness calms them immediately. It also gives you something to hold.

  4. Support the body, don’t squeeze

    One hand under the belly, fingers around the wings but not pressing them. Birds have air sacs under the ribs — you can suffocate them by squeezing.

  5. Straight into the box

    Don’t hold it to show anyone. Don’t take a photo. Box it, close the lid.

If it’s a raven and it’s alert, don’t try to handle it yourself. Call us and stay with the bird from a distance. An adult raven that can still move is genuinely dangerous, and an injured raven that escapes into traffic is worse off than before.

Please don’t

Nine things that make it worse.

✕

Give it bread, milk, or birdseed

✕

Give it water by syringe or dropper

✕

Put it in a wire cage or a hamster cage

✕

Keep it in the same room as a cat or dog

✕

Apply human antiseptic, oil, or ointment

✕

Try to splint a wing or leg yourself

✕

Let children hold it “to say goodbye”

✕

Keep it overnight “to see how it does”

✕

Release it somewhere unfamiliar

Outside our area

    Corvallis, OR · (541) 745-5324 · Birds and small mammals, Benton County.
